# Screening round guide

The first call of a process. It decides whether the hiring manager sees me at all.

## Who is in the room

A recruiter or talent-acquisition person, in-house or from an agency, rarely HR proper. An agency recruiter works for a client company; when the context does not name the client, finding out who it is comes first.

## What they score

1. Career logic and motivation: why this move, why now, why this company.
2. Level fit: whether my experience matches the level of the role.
3. Clear communication: whether I can explain my background in two minutes, and listen.
4. Logistics: location and remote work, work authorization, contract form, relocation.
5. Timeline: notice period, start date, and other processes I am in.
6. Pay against their band. Pay outside the band is one of the most common reasons a screen ends in a rejection.

It is not a technical round, but recruiters often read a technology checklist from the posting and note yes, no or partly for each item. They do not judge code or design.

## Format and length

15 to 45 minutes by phone or video. Sometimes an AI note-taker records the call and the hiring manager reads its notes, so clean, complete sentences matter more than usual. The call usually ends with my questions and an explanation of the process.

## Likely questions, ranked

1. Tell me about yourself.
2. Why are you looking for something new, and why now?
3. Why this company, and why this role?
4. Where are you based, and how would the location work: remote, hybrid or a move?
5. Are you authorized to work there, and would you take employment or a contract?
6. The technology checklist: "Have you worked with ...?" for the main items in the posting.
7. A project you are proud of, or a recent challenge.
8. What are your salary expectations?
9. What is your notice period, when could you start, and are you in other processes?
10. Do you have any questions for me?

Also likely when it applies: a title above or below the level of the role, a long time with one employer, a career break.

## What to prepare

- Pitch: the 90-second version, and the 30-second version too when the call is 30 minutes or shorter.
- Recurring answers: why now, why this company, and any the posting raises.
- Gaps and bridges: one for every checklist item I lack.
- Stories: one project story and one backup, each told by its Short telling when it has one, otherwise by its Telling: a recruiter is not an engineer.
- Logistics: every line.
- Pay: the method, and the line worked out for this job.

## Cards

Pitch; why this company; why now; logistics (location, authorization, contract form); tech checklist; one story; pay; notice and start; my questions. Add a card for a title, tenure or career-break question when it applies.

## Mock interviewer

- Persona: a talent-acquisition specialist at the company, or an agency recruiter when the context says so: friendly and businesslike.
- Behaviour: starts with a minute of small talk, then introduces themselves and the company in a few sentences. Follows up the way recruiters do, asking for a concrete example or for what an answer means in practice.
- Question plan: seven or eight of the ranked questions, mostly in the ranked order, with one or two checklist items from the posting.
- Press on: logistics and pay. When I dodge the pay question once, ask once more, politely, for a range. Reveal a band only when I ask for one; then give a plausible band for the role and the location, from the research and in the market's period, and see how I handle it.
- Level: the questions of a recruiter: no algorithms, no system design, and technology only as a checklist.
- Clock: a compressed rehearsal of the real call, whose length goes in the header: 15 to 20 minutes, about eight questions with their follow-ups.
- Ending: a few minutes for my questions, then what happens next in the process, as the research found it, and goodbye.

## Feedback focus

- The pitch: its length, its order (where I am now, how I got there, why this role) and whether it lands in 90 seconds.
- Logistics answered plainly, without hedging.
- Pay: their range asked for first, a range and not a number when pressed, no apology, never "whatever you think is fair".
- Gaps stated as facts with the bridge, never apologized for.
- The red flags a recruiter writes down: dodging pay, sounding unsure about the location, a bad word about anyone, overselling, answers that run long.

## Research focus

- The company: what it sells and to whom, its size, its ownership or funding, its recent news.
- The reported process: how many rounds, and what comes after the screen.
- Pay for the role and level in the job's location: ranges published in postings, salary data sites and national salary surveys; and for the net estimate, the take-home file of that country (see Research in `prep.md`).
- The company's remote and relocation policy, when it publishes one.
