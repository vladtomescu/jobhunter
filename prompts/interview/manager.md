# Hiring manager round guide

The conversation with the person I would report to. It decides whether they want me on their team.

## Who is in the room

The engineering manager or team lead of the team the role sits in. Sometimes a senior engineer from the team joins.

## What they score

1. Depth on past projects: what was built, why, and what I did myself.
2. My impact: results I can put a number or an outcome on.
3. How I work: ownership, code review, testing, releases and on-call, process.
4. Motivation and role fit: why this team, what I want from the role, what I need from a manager.
5. How I work with people: disagreement, feedback, mentoring.
6. Sometimes light technical questions about the team's stack or its problems.

The manager also sells the role. Listen for what the team needs and what worries the manager, and connect my answers to it.

## Format and length

45 to 60 minutes by video. Often behavioral questions, answered with stories. Follow-ups ask "why" and "what did you do yourself". The last part is for my questions about the team, which nobody answers better than the manager.

## Likely questions, ranked

1. Walk me through your current role and what you own.
2. Tell me about a project you are proud of, followed by a deep dive into its decisions and trade-offs.
3. What was your own part, and what did the team do?
4. How do you work day to day: code review, testing, releases, on-call?
5. Tell me about a disagreement with a colleague or a manager, and how it ended.
6. Tell me about a mistake or a failure, and what you changed after it.
7. Why this team, and why now?
8. What do you need from a manager to do your best work?
9. A light technical question about the stack or a problem the team has.
10. What questions do you have for me?

## What to prepare

- Pitch: the 2-minute version, and the 30-second version too when the call is 30 minutes or shorter.
- Stories: four or five, covering a project with depth, ownership, a disagreement, a failure and mentoring, each with its follow-ups.
- Reference systems: the summary, the design and the trade-offs of the main one, at the depth a manager asks for.
- Recurring answers: why now, why this role, what I need from a manager.
- Gaps and bridges for the main requirements of the posting.

## Cards

Pitch; my current role, in the numbers my files hold; three or four story cards in STAR keywords; how I work (review, tests, releases, on-call); why this team; gaps; my questions (the team, how work arrives, what success looks like after six months, next steps).

## Mock interviewer

- Persona: the engineering manager of the team the role sits in.
- Behaviour: friendly and curious. Digs into every project answer with "why" and "what did you do yourself". Sells the role briefly when I ask about the team.
- Question plan: six to eight of the ranked questions, starting with the current role and one deep dive.
- Press on: my own part against the team's; the decisions and what they gave up; evidence for each claim; what I would do differently.
- Level: a conversation between engineers. Light technical depth only, no whiteboard.
- Clock: about 20 minutes.
- Ending: time for my questions, a short word on what the team is working on, the next steps, goodbye.

## Feedback focus

- "I" against "we": is my own part clear?
- Concrete results, with the numbers my files hold.
- Depth under follow-up: does the story hold when pressed?
- Consistency with what the company already has: the kit, the letter, the earlier rounds.
- Questions that show interest in the team and its work.

## Research focus

- The team or product area the role sits in, from the posting and the company's pages.
- The engineering blog, talks or open-source work, for the stack and the problems the team has.
- The reported format of the hiring manager round.

## Pay

Not by default. When the context says pay may come up, a short Pay section with the ask-first question and the prepared range, worked out as `prep.md` says, and the market table only when no earlier round covered pay.
