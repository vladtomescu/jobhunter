# Fit round guide

The behavioral and values round, often the final one. "Final" names a place in the process, not a content: when the final round is a full loop of technical rounds, prepare it with the tech and system design guides instead, as a joint round.

## Who is in the room

A product manager or product owner, team leads, peers, and sometimes a founder, the CTO or another senior leader.

## What they score

1. Values and culture: how I work with people and what I care about.
2. Behavioral answers told as STAR stories: the Situation and the Task in about 20 percent of the answer, the Action in about 60 percent, told with "I" and not "we", and a concrete Result, followed by what I would do differently.
3. Collaboration and conflict.
4. Working with product: how requirements arrive, pushing back, explaining technical debt to people who are not engineers, prioritizing.
5. Product sense: why the product matters to the people who use it.
6. Leadership signals: ownership, mentoring, raising the bar, influence without authority.

## Format and length

30 to 60 minutes by video, sometimes several short conversations in a row.

## Likely questions, ranked

1. Tell me about yourself, in short.
2. Tell me about a conflict with a colleague, and how you resolved it.
3. Tell me about a time you failed or made a mistake.
4. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a product decision.
5. How do you explain technical debt to someone who is not an engineer?
6. Tell me about a time you took ownership of something outside your job.
7. Tell me about a time you changed a decision without the authority to make it.
8. How do you prioritize when everything is urgent?
9. What kind of team do you work best in, and what do you value?
10. Why this company, and what questions do you have for us?

## What to prepare

- Pitch: the 90-second version, and the 30-second version too when the call is 30 minutes or shorter.
- Stories: a bank of six to eight that covers conflict, failure, influence, ownership and cross-functional work, each one with what I would do differently. Each is told by its Short telling when it has one, otherwise by its Telling, since product people and leaders are often not engineers.
- The company's published values, each matched to a story.
- Recurring answers: why this company, and what I value in a team.

## Cards

Pitch; one card per story theme, in STAR keywords; the company's values matched to my stories; working with product (requirements, pushing back, technical debt, priorities); my questions last (how decisions are made, what the team is proud of, the next steps).

## Mock interviewer

- Persona: the product manager the role works with, or a senior leader when the context says so.
- Behaviour: warm and conversational, then probes behind each story: what did you do yourself, how did the other person see it, what would you do differently.
- Question plan: five to seven behavioral questions across the themes, at most two on the same theme.
- Press on: "we" where "I" belongs, a missing result, a lesson that sounds rehearsed.
- Level: no technical depth. Product, people and judgment.
- Clock: about 20 minutes.
- Ending: time for my questions, what happens next in the process, goodbye.

## Feedback focus

- The STAR balance: the Action at about 60 percent, a concrete Result.
- "I" against "we".
- Consistency across the stories, and with the earlier rounds.
- Fit with the company's values, shown in the stories rather than claimed.
- Leadership signals.

## Research focus

- The company's published values and how it says it works: its culture, careers and engineering pages.
- The product and the people who use it, and the recent news.
- The reported format of the final round.

## Pay

Only when this is the final round: a short Pay section in case an offer conversation starts, with the ask-first question, the prepared range and, marked private, the walk-away, taken from the job's pay line as `prep.md` says. Otherwise none.
